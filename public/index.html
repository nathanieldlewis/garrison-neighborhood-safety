03 / SOURCES & METHOD

Read the counts with their limits in view

This is an independent community analysis of public data, not a CMPD publication or a prediction of personal risk.

3.01

One report, one classification

The daily view counts distinct incident-report IDs. CMPD assigns the highest NIBRS offense to each report. Lower-ranked offenses within the same incident are not separately counted here.

3.02

Report date, not occurrence date

Windows use the date reported, in Eastern Time, through the previous completed day. Comparisons use an equal number of days ending one calendar year earlier. Exact dates appear with the figures.

3.03

Crime totals exclude three groups

Reports marked unfounded are removed first, followed by non-criminal 800-series codes and unrecognized codes. Unrecognized codes remain visible in the reconciliation until reviewed.

3.04

Nearby does not mean on the property

The radius query uses the coordinates published by CMPD. Public locations may be generalized. Counts cannot establish which business or residential parcel an incident originated from.

3.05

A fresh check is not complete coverage

The latest citywide report date is not a certified cutoff. Reports can be delayed, revised or reclassified. Failed updates retain the last good snapshot; a warning appears when it becomes stale.

3.06

No personal incident details

Only aggregate counts are published. The site omits names, ages, unit numbers, incident narratives, report IDs, exact incident locations, private correspondence and Board records.

Category definitions and the firearm-data limitation

Violent crime: NIBRS 09A, 11A, 11B, 11C, 120 and 13A. Vehicle crime: 240, 23F and 23G. Drugs and equipment: 35A and 35B. Burglary: 220. Vandalism: 290. Other theft and fraud: remaining 23-series and 26-series codes, plus 250, 270 and 280. Other recognized criminal codes are grouped separately.

Daily firearm totals are not available from this schema. The public feed does not provide the weapon-level detail needed to reproduce the archive's gun-assault and gun-violation counts. Weapon-law code 520 is not automatically treated as firearm crime.

The archive's broader grouping of violence and sex offenses differs from the daily view. Historical totals are not appended to the live series. A difference in counts is not proof that an intervention caused a change.
